 Comment Venomous! Synonymy: Vipera ammodytes transcaucasiana is considered as a valid species by several authors (like JOGER, pers. comm., Nilson et al. 1999), but not by others (e.g. BARAN 1998, DAVID et al. 1999, GÖÇMEN et al. 2014, WALLACH et al. 2014: 786). V. pontica is a hybrid between sympatric V. kaznakovi and V. transcaucasiana (see V. kaznakovi). We follow recent authors in considering it as a subspecies of ammodytes. Krecsák et al 2024 synonymized V. a. montandoni with ammodytes, but Krecsák et al 2025 redefined ammodytes s. str. to contain what used to be montandoni but NOT the western populations which are now V. a. illyrica. Dufresnes et al. 2024 have revalidated meridionalis as valid species and their phylogenetic analysis suggested that transcaucasiana may be a synonym. But they did not explicitly synonymize the two, so we keep transcaucasiana as a subspecies for now. Subspecies: Vipera ammodytes transcaucasiana is considered as species by several authors (like JOGER in prep., Nilson et al. in press), but not by others (e.g. BARAN 1998; U. Joger, pers. comm.). It is included here as valid species. Most authors don’t seem to recognize V. a. gregorwallneri and synonymize it with V. a. ammodytes (e.g. GOLAY et al. 1993). URSENBACHER et al. (2008) synonymized gregorwallneri and ruffoi with ammodytes. The validity of V. a. montandoni was questioned recently based of on morphological characters (Christov & Beshkov 1999, Tomović & Džukić 2003). Garrigues et al. (2005) found that V. a. montandoni is phylogenetically closely related to V. a. meridionalis, thus these authors proposed to regard the former as a synonym of V. a. meridionalis. Tomović (2006) reconfirm the validity of V. a. montandoni, based on morphometric analysis of five mersitic and qualitative characters on 922 specimens. However, KRECSÁK et al. 2024 synonymize montandoni again with ammodytes, that the type of ammodytes is actually not ammodytes and that illyrica has priority over ammodytes which needs to be renamed if not protected. Type genus: Rhinaspis is the type genus of the subfamily Rhinaspinae REUSS 1933. Type locality: Mertens & Müller (1928) proposed the restriction of the type locality of Vipera ammodytes ammodytes (Linné) to Illyria. Schwarz (1936) proposed to restrict the type locality to Zara (Zadar; Croatia) without clarification or explanation. Later, the restricted type locality, Zadar, was followed by Mertens & Müller (1940), Mertens & Wermuth (1960), Saint Girons (1978), McDiarmid et al. (1999), and others. Bruno (1968) restricted the type locality to near Castello Nuovo di Duino (Trieste, Venezia Giulia NW), NE Italy, noting the holotype as male uv., 95 SCL-ZMUU. Krecsák et al 2024 believe that the type is from the surroundings of Belgrad village Distribution data still reflecting inclusion of V. transcaucasiana. V. a. ammodytes hybridized with V. aspis atra in captivity (FAORO 1986), with V. aspis zinnikeri (OBERMAYER 1967), and with V. aspis aspis (SCHWEIZER 1941). Has been erroneously reported from S Czechoslovakia (but doesn’t occur there fide Hegner, pers. comm.). Reports from Hungary may be based on historical reports when parts of Romania belonged to Hungary (and may therefore represent records from Romania). Not in Armenia fide Jeff Ettling (pers. comm., 2 April 2015). For a map see Sindaco et al. 2013. Welch 1994 listed meridionalis from Albania, Greece, S Serbia, Romania, S Bulgaria, and European Turkey. Not in the Middle East fide IUCN assessment. For a map of subspecies see Hempel et al. 2018 (Fig. 1) but in that map montandoni needs to be replaced by ammodytes and ammodytes should be replaced by illyrica, fide KRECSÁK et al. 2024, 2025 Genome: Laustsen et al. 2025. NCBI taxonID: 235552 [transcaucasiana] RDB speciesID: 20001 [transcaucasiana]  
 Etymology From Greek ammodutēs = sand-dweller (Bodson 2014), from ammos = “sand”, and dutes = “burrower” or “diver”. However, the name Sand Viper seems somewhat inapproriate as the species rarely occurs in truly sandy habitats. V. a. buchholzi was named after Karl F. Buchholz for being the first to report the presence of Vipera ammodytes on the island of Antiparos and for his important contribution to the knowledge of Reptiles of the Aegean Islands. V. a. montandoni was named after the collector of the type, M.A. Montandon.
